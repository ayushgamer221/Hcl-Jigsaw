THE PROBLEM

Fixed-timer signals give every road the same green time, whatever is waiting. When one road is busy and the cross road is nearly empty, vehicles pile up on the busy road while green time is spent on empty lanes. In dense Indian city corridors this wastes time and fuel.

OUR SOLUTION

The adaptive controller reads the current queue on each approach, adds a waiting-time priority so no road is ignored, and gives green to the movement group with the greater demand. Minimum green, maximum green, amber and all-red clearance always apply. It never sees future arrivals.

Any improvement is reported only from measured simulation runs. No improvement figure is assumed.

MODEL ASSUMPTIONS

  • Time step 1 s. Arrivals per approach: each second a vehicle arrives with probability rate/60 (Bernoulli approximation of a Poisson process), from a seeded generator.
  • Two movement groups (North–South, East–West); only one group is green at a time. Green → amber → all-red → other group.
  • Each green approach discharges one vehicle per headway. A vehicle counts as served when it crosses the stop line; its departure is 3 s later.
  • Full queues block arrivals; blocked vehicles are counted separately. No warm-up period is used; both controllers start with North–South green and empty queues.

ABOUT
ADAPTIVE ALGORITHM
  1. Demand score of a group = vehicles queued on its two approaches + 0.1 × longest head-of-queue wait (seconds). The wait term stops any road being ignored.
  2. No decision before the minimum green has elapsed. Green always ends at the maximum green.
  3. After the minimum, green ends early if the other group's score exceeds 1.25 × the current group's score + 2.
  4. Amber and all-red clearance always run before switching. Ties and starts favour North–South.
  5. Only current queues and waits are used; future arrivals are never known.


FAIRNESS
A paired comparison feeds both controllers the same pre-generated arrival sequence and applies identical capacity, headway, crossing, amber, all-red and end-of-run rules. Only the green-time decision differs.


LIMITATIONS
  • Results are simulated, not measured on real roads. Single straight-through lane per approach; no turns, pedestrians or incidents.
  • No statistical significance test is implemented; trial summaries are descriptive only.
  • Adaptive control may lose to fixed timing in some scenarios; the application reports this honestly.
